Erasing and redrawing only what changed

Each change, timed with the same schedule the sim and the motors run. The old way wiped the whole drawing area and drew everything again.

How: strokes already on the board within 1.5 mm are kept. Four ways to wipe the rest are timed (follow each stroke, rows over only the cells with ink, a zigzag over its box, the whole area) and the fastest is used. Anything kept that the eraser brushes is redrawn, only where it was touched. Every plan is then played through the SG92R sim onto an ink raster and checked against what the board should show.

The order strokes are drawn in

Up to 12 strokes, the order is the proven optimum (Held–Karp, 13 ms for 12 strokes). Above that, nearest-first then 2-opt and Or-opt, which lands within 1% to 4% of the optimum on average, against 9% to 16% for nearest-first alone.

Extra pen-up travel against the true best, on random strokes. On real drawings it cuts pen-up travel by 9% to 16% (the cat 396 to 332 mm, the Oxford crest 648 to 560 mm) but only 0.1 to 0.3 s of time, because drawing dominates. The big savings are in what gets wiped and redrawn.

Is the simulation what the motors get?

Yes. The keyframes the Arduino receives were replayed through a line-for-line copy of the firmware's 50 Hz interpolation and compared with the sim's servo commands at every frame.

All well inside the servo's own 1° dead band. Checking this found three real faults, now fixed: the fallback protocol sent uncorrected angles; the base servo ran into its end stop in the bottom right of the drawing (the zero offsets now centre each joint's range: mount the base so its middle points 32° up from the board's bottom edge, and the elbow so its middle is 100° round from link 1); and between wipes the pen swung over to the nib side and back.

Better drawings

A sharper instruction did more than a bigger model: name the subject's distinctive features and reproduce known logos faithfully. With it, Gemini's flash models draw a recognisable University of Oxford crest. Only the lite model has free quota left today, and its crest is weak.

The free tier gives 20 requests a day per flash model and none for the pro model. The app tries the pro model first, then the flash models, then lite, and skips any model that's out of quota or busy, so turning on billing for the Gemini project makes the pro model answer with no other change.

Talking to the arm

Each simulation on the left is a board and a conversation, saved as you go. The chat changes the board: draw something, change it, wipe it, or play noughts and crosses (tap a square or say where). The computer player never loses: tested against all 521 ways a person can play.

How these were measured
  • node app/tools/optimize-test.mjs: stroke order against Held–Karp on random strokes; every change planned four ways, timed with makeJob, played through the SG92R sim (600°/s, 1° dead band) onto a 0.5 mm ink raster and compared with the target.
  • node app/tools/verify-motors.mjs: keyframes from arm.keyframes() replayed through a copy of frame() in firmware/clockwork/clockwork.ino, compared every 20 ms within the 1 ms rounding of keyframe times.
  • Gemini: the same prompt to each model on 24 Oct 2026, timed end to end; quota limits read from the API's own 429 responses.
  • Noughts and crosses: every sequence of human moves played against the computer.
  • Times are for the design dimensions (180 mm links, 165 mm pen at 56°); the arm isn't measured yet.
