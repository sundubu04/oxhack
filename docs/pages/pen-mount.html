Four ways compared

Same pen, same SG92R servo, clamp where you set it. The shaft height is the only thing that changes, and it fixes everything else.

Why this angle: the same pen at every angle

Lower angles slide less at each touch, turn the servo less and keep the shaft lower, but bring the barrel's edge closer to the board and meet it with more of the eraser's rim. So the best is the lowest angle that still keeps the barrel clear by the margin. Up to about 65° works too, with a longer slide.

How to attach it

  1. Servo at the end of the forearm, shaft along the forearm. The pen then swings across the forearm, so ink and eraser touch to either side of the arm, not ahead of it or behind it. The arm's reach is the same for both.
  2. Shaft 68 mm off the board. A ball caster under the bracket, on a post of that height, keeps the distance fixed as the arm moves.
  3. Clamp the pen at its balance point, a printed collar with a screw, fixed to the servo's own horn. Balanced, its weight puts no load on the servo on an upright board.
  4. Trim the eraser felt to 56°, or glue a felt pad on a printed wedge at that angle, so it wipes with its whole face rather than its rim.
  5. Find the three angles on the real arm. Start from the numbers above, then nudge draw and erase until the nib and the felt just bend.

What changes in the app

The nib and the eraser now touch 35 mm either side of the shaft, not at its tip. Clockwork needs that offset per tool so it steers the nib for drawing and the felt for erasing. The three tilt angles go into Arm setup as they are.

How the numbers are worked out
  • The pen is a straight bar clamped at a point, turning about a shaft parallel to the board. With the shaft at height d, a pen end at distance r from the clamp meets the board at angle asin(d / r).
  • Servo turn from ink to eraser is the ink angle plus the eraser angle. Time at the SG92R's 0.1 s per 60° (4.8 V datasheet).
  • The barrel's edge clears the board while nib length × sin(angle) is more than barrel radius × cos(angle).
  • The slide while pressing is how far the nib moves along the board between first touch and the press depth. It shows up as a short tail at each stroke start and end.
  • Servo load per newton of pen force is the clamp-to-nib distance × cos(angle). SG92R stall torque is 2.5 kg·cm, 24.5 N·cm.
  • The old spec's 15° rocker came from iRobot Root, whose arm carries a pen and a separate eraser that both face the board. A single marker with its eraser at the far end can't work that way.
  • Pen size is a placeholder until measured: whiteboard markers vary.
