Drawing arm — build spec

Serial two-link arm that draws a 20 cm clock centred on a 35 × 35 cm whiteboard, with a rolling-ball tip and a tilt marker. Sized for small motors. For 3D printing with motor slots.

OxHack · hardware clockv1 · check motor model before cutting slotslive simulator

The two numbers to print to L1 = L2 = 160 mm axis-to-axis (shoulder→elbow and elbow→pen). Base pivot centred 30 mm below the board. 200 mm clock, centred on the board with ~7.5 cm of margin all round.

Dimensioned layout (front view, looking at the board)

Both links are the same length. The arm bends with the elbow near 90° across the clock (dexterity ≥ 0.43), well clear of the folded and stretched singularities. Reach is 320 mm; the clock needs ~305 mm of it, leaving margin.

End effector

At the tip of link 2: a ball transfer / caster (10–14 mm ball) rides on the board and sets a constant height. A micro tilt servo swings a double-ended marker holder through three positions — draw (−14°), lift (−2°), erase (+16°). No lift motor.

Print notes

  • Stiffness is accuracy. Any flex in a link bends the whole lever, so give each link a box or I-beam rib, not a flat bar. PLA is fine; PETG if it'll sit in sun/heat.
  • Keep links light. Low infill with a stiff shell lowers inertia and sag, which keeps the motor torque low — the whole point of the short arm.
  • Joints: press-fit the 608 at the elbow (bore ~21.8 mm, tune for your printer). Couple links to motor shafts with a printed horn to the spline (25T / 20T) or a clamp onto the 5 mm D-shaft — avoid relying on friction alone.
  • Orientation: print links flat so layers run along the length (resists bending); add chamfers at the joints to avoid layer splitting.
  • Zero reference: add a small hard stop or witness mark at each joint so the firmware can home to a known angle.

For the firmware

The arm is controlled as a 2-link planar arm: base angle θ₁, elbow angle θ₂, plus the tilt. Origin at the base pivot, x right, y up, angles degrees CCW from +x, elbow relative to link 1.

L1 = L2 = 160 mm
// target pen (x,y) -> angles
r = hypot(x,y);  if (r > 320 || r < 0) out_of_reach;
c2 = (x*x + y*y - 160² - 160²) / (2*160*160);
θ2 = atan2(+sqrt(1-c2²), c2);              // elbow up
θ1 = atan2(y,x) - atan2(160*sin θ2, 160 + 160*cos θ2);
tilt_deg = { draw:-14, lift:-2, erase:+16 }
