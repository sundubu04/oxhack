Drawing arm — build spec

Serial two-link arm that draws a 20 cm clock centred on a 35 × 35 cm whiteboard, with a rolling-ball tip and a tilt marker. PLA links, 3× TowerPro SG92R servos. For 3D printing with motor slots.

OxHack · hardware clockv2 · PLA links, SG92R servoslive simulator

The two numbers to print to L1 = L2 = 180 mm axis-to-axis (shoulder→elbow and elbow→pen). Base pivot centred 40 mm below the board (a compact bracket). 200 mm clock, centred on the board with ~7.5 cm of margin all round.

Dimensioned layout (front view, looking at the board)

Both links are the same length. The arm bends with the elbow near 90° across the clock (dexterity ≥ 0.43), well clear of the folded and stretched singularities. Reach is 360 mm; the clock needs ~315 mm of it, leaving margin.

End effector

Changed 24 Oct: the earlier ±16° rocker can't work with one marker whose eraser is on its far end, because the barrel hits the board first. Use the 60° mount from the pen mount study. The tilt servo sits at the tip of link 2 with its shaft along the link, about 61 mm above the board, and clamps the marker at its balance point. It turns the pen to draw −62°, lift −55°, erase +62° from lying flat (28°, 35°, 152° on the servo). A ball caster on a 61 mm post keeps that height. Numbers are for a 140 mm marker: re-run the study with yours.

SG92R motor slots & torque

Three TowerPro SG92R micro servos — shoulder, elbow and tilt. Same slot for all three.

SG92RValue
Body pocket23 × 12.2 × 27 mm (cut ~23.5 × 12.8 mm, 27 mm deep)

Output shaft~4.8 mm spline, ~20T (takes SG90 horns)
Torque · speed · weight2.5 kg·cm @ 4.8 V · 0.1 s/60° · 9 g

Orient each servo so its output shaft sits on the joint axis. Couple the link to the servo through a printed horn screwed to an SG90 arm — don't trust the spline friction alone. Leave a channel for the 3-wire lead.

Torque is fine; accuracy is the limit.The ~60 g PLA arm needs ~0.9 kg·cm at the shoulder — 35% of the SG92R's 2.5 kg·cm stall on a vertical board, ~0.34 kg·cm at the elbow, trivial flat on a table. But bare SG92Rs give ~6–7 mm tip error, so the clock reads hand-drawn (fine for the Baas look). A 3:1 reduction later brings it to ~2 mm.

Print notes

  • Stiffness is accuracy. Any flex in a link bends the whole lever, so give each link a box or I-beam rib, not a flat bar. PLA is fine; PETG if it'll sit in sun/heat.
  • Keep links light. Low infill with a stiff shell lowers inertia and sag, which keeps the SG92R torque demand low (it's already only ~35% of stall). PLA flexes ~0.5 mm per link under load on a vertical board; the box rib cuts that.
  • Run flat on a table if you can. Gravity then acts out of the drawing plane and the ball carries the tip, so the servos only fight friction — least sag, least drift, least torque.
  • Joints: press-fit the 608 at the elbow (bore ~21.8 mm, tune for your printer). Couple links to motor shafts with a printed horn to the spline (25T / 20T) or a clamp onto the 5 mm D-shaft — avoid relying on friction alone.
  • Orientation: print links flat so layers run along the length (resists bending); add chamfers at the joints to avoid layer splitting.
  • Zero reference: add a small hard stop or witness mark at each joint so the firmware can home to a known angle.

For the firmware

The arm is controlled as a 2-link planar arm: base angle θ₁, elbow angle θ₂, plus the tilt. Origin at the base pivot, x right, y up, angles degrees CCW from +x, elbow relative to link 1.

L1 = L2 = 180 mm
// target pen (x,y) -> angles
r = hypot(x,y);  if (r > 360 || r < 0) out_of_reach;
c2 = (x*x + y*y - 180² - 180²) / (2*180*180);
θ2 = atan2(+sqrt(1-c2²), c2);              // elbow up
θ1 = atan2(y,x) - atan2(180*sin θ2, 180 + 180*cos θ2);
tilt_deg = { draw:-62, lift:-55, erase:+62 }   // from the pen lying flat; servo 28, 35, 152
// the nib touches 35 mm to one side of the link's tip, the eraser 35 mm to the other:
// aim with L2' = hypot(180, 35) and subtract atan2(±35, 180) from θ2
